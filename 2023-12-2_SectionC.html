短文一。
Passage 1.
人工智能代理在我们的生活中扮演着越来越有影响力的角色。
Artificial intelligence agents play ever more influential roles in our lives.
从推荐新朋友到推荐购物，它们什么都能做。
They do everything from suggesting new friends to recommending purchases.
它们甚至开始帮我们开车。
They're even beginning to drive our cars.
人们预期它们会接手的另一个角色，是在商业交易或法律纠纷中代表我们进行谈判。
Another role that they are expected to take over is negotiating on our behalf in commercial transactions or legal disputes.
因此，了解使用人工智能代理是否会影响我们谈判的方式就很重要。
So, it's important to know whether using an artificial intelligence agent might affect how we negotiate.
研究表明确实会。
Research indicates that it does.
在一项新研究中，参与者被要求想象自己正在为一件对他们很重要的东西谈判，比如一套房子。
In a new study, participants were told to imagine that they were negotiating for something important to them, like a house.
接着，他们被告知要么自己亲自谈判，要么设置一个人工智能代理替他们谈判。
Next, they were told either that they would negotiate for themselves, or they would program an artificial intelligence agent to negotiate for them.
随后，参与者完成了一份问卷，说明在谈判中他们希望自己、或希望他们的代理表现得多么强硬、多么有欺骗性、多么友善或反之。
Participants then completed a survey indicating how tough, deceptive, and pleasant or otherwise they wanted to be, or wanted their agent to be, in the negotiations.
例如，参与者可以选择强硬，也就是一开口就提出远远高于自己愿意接受的条件。
For example, participants could choose to be tough by making an opening demand far greater than what they'd be willing to accept.
他们也可以选择向对手表达同情，以显得友善。
They could also choose to express sympathy with their opponent to appear pleasant.
但他们也可以表明，自己或自己的代理会策略性地向对手表达愤怒，以取得优势。
But they could also indicate that they, or their agent, would strategically express anger toward the opponent to gain advantage.
或者，他们可以选择表露出对这次会面的不满，好让另一方以为他们正在失去兴趣。
Or, they could opt to convey dissatisfaction with the encounter so that the other party would think they were losing interest.
这两者都是欺骗性策略的例子。
These are both examples of deceptive strategies.
当被指派一个代理替自己谈判时，参与者更愿意使用欺骗性策略。
Participants were more willing to employ deceptive strategies when assigned an agent to negotiate on their behalf.
短文二。
Passage 2.
一项新研究考察了为期 7 周的健康烹饪课程的效果。
New research has studied the effects of a seven-week healthy cooking course.
研究人员测量了该课程对烹饪信心和自评心理健康的影响。
Academics measured the programme's effect on cooking confidence and self-perceived mental health.
研究人员还测量了参与者对烹饪的总体满意度以及与饮食相关的行为。
Researchers also measured participants' overall satisfaction around cooking and diet-related behaviours.
课程参与者的总体健康状况有了显著改善。
Course participants saw significant improvements in general health.
课程结束后不久，他们还报告说心理健康和主观活力都有所改善。
They also reported improved mental health and subjective vitality immediately after the programme.
课程结束 6 个月后，这些好处依然存在。
These benefits remained six months after the completion of the course.
研究人员此前已发现，多吃水果和蔬菜与长期心理健康的改善之间存在关联。
Researchers have previously found a link between eating more fruits and vegetables and improved longer-term mental health.
这意味着，当前研究中的参与者可能正是因为饮食改善而感觉更好。
This would imply that the participants in the current study may have felt better due to improved diet.
然而，研究表明，即使参与者在完成课程后报告的饮食并没有改变，他们的心理健康仍有所改善。
However, the study showed participants' mental health improved even if their reported diet did not change after completing the programme.
此外，超重或肥胖的参与者和体重处于健康范围的参与者，所获得的心理健康益处是相同的。
Also, the mental health benefits were equal among participants who were overweight or obese and those in a healthy weight range.
这表明，烹饪信心和对烹饪的满意度和心理健康方面的益处之间存在关联。
This suggests a link between cooking confidence and satisfaction around cooking and mental health benefits.
谁从学做饭中获益最多？
Who benefits most from learning to cook?
性别在其中起了作用。
Gender plays a part.
课程开始时，77% 的女性参与者对烹饪有信心，而男性只有 23% 有信心。
At the start of the programme, 77% of female participants were confident about cooking, but just 23% of males were confident.
课程结束时，两类参与者的烹饪信心和技能已经不相上下。
At the end of the programme, cooking confidence and skills were equal across both counterparts.
这种信心的变化可能会让家庭烹饪中的性别比例趋于平衡。
This change in confidence could lead to a gender balance in home cooking.
这反过来又能减少不健康、高热量加工食品的摄入。
This, in turn, could reduce consumption of unhealthy, high-calorie processed meals.
短文三。什么是个人空间？
Passage 3 What is personal space?
我们常把它想成围绕在我们周围的一个无形空间气泡，别人一旦进入就会让人感到不适。
We often think of it as an invisible bubble of space surrounding us that others can't enter without causing discomfort.
然而，研究表明，我们实际上拥有大小不同的气泡。
Research shows, however, that we actually have bubbles of different sizes.
这些气泡各自适用于不同的人群。
Each of these bubbles applies to a different set of people.
最小的区域叫做亲密空间，从我们身体向各个方向向外延伸 18 英寸。
The smallest zone, called intimate space, extends outward from our bodies 18 inches in every direction.
只有家人、宠物和最亲密的朋友才能进入。
Only family, pets, and one's closest friends may enter it.
仅仅是熟人进入我们的亲密空间，也会让我们感到不适。
A mere acquaintance entering our intimate space makes us uncomfortable.
下一个叫做个人空间的气泡，范围是从 1.5 英尺到 4 英尺远。
Next is the bubble called personal space, extending from 1.5 feet to 4 feet away.
朋友和熟人可以自在地待在这个区域里，尤其是在非正式交谈时，但陌生人则被排斥在外。
Friends and acquaintances can comfortably occupy this zone, especially during informal conversations, but strangers are forbidden.
从我们身边 4 英尺到 12 英尺远的地方是社交空间。
Extending from 4 to 12 feet away from us is social space.
在这里，人们可以自在地与新认识的人或完全陌生的人进行日常社交往来。
Here, people feel comfortable conducting routine social interactions with new acquaintances or total strangers.
不管怎样，这些是美国人气泡的平均大小。
Those are the average sizes of Americans' personal bubbles, anyway.
重要的是要记住，个人空间会因文化和情境而异。
It is important to keep in mind that personal space varies depending on culture and context.
此外，人与人之间还存在显著的个体差异。
Furthermore, there are significant individual differences.
正如我们所知，个人气泡直径在文化或个体上的差异，往往是造成不适的原因。
As we all know, cultural or individual differences in personal bubble diameters are all too often the cause of discomfort.
这些个人气泡是如何形成的？
How did these personal bubbles arise?
根据研究，我们在 3 到 4 岁左右开始形成个人的空间感。
According to research, we begin to develop our individual sense of personal space around age 3 or 4.
我们气泡的大小在青少年时期就固定下来。
The sizes of our bubbles are fixed by our teens.
这些气泡由大脑中与恐惧相关的区域构建和监控。
These bubbles are constructed and monitored by the brain region involved in fear.
