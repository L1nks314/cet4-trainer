第 1 篇 · 第 46–50 题

6 段 · 5 题
P1

Lao Zi once said, "Care about what other people think and you will always be their prisoner."

P2

People-pleasing, or seeking self-worth through others' approval, is unproductive and an exhausting way to go through life. Why do we allow what others think of us to have so much power over how we feel about ourselves? If it's true that you can't please all people all of the time, wouldn't it make sense to stop trying?

P3

Unfortunately, sense often isn't driving our behavior. For social beings who desire love and belonging, wanting to be liked, and caring about the effect we have on others, is healthy and allows us to make connections. However, where we get into trouble is when our self-worth is dependent upon whether we win someone's approval or not.

P4

This need to be liked can be traced back to when we were children and were completely dependent on others to take care of us: Small children are not just learning how to walk and communicate, they are also trying to learn how the world works. We learn about who we are and what is expected of us based on interactions with others, so, to a four-year-old, if Mommy or Daddy doesn't like him or her, there is the danger that they will abandon them. We need to understand that when we desperately want someone to approve of us, it's being driven by that little kid part of us that is still terrified of abandonment.

P5

As you become more capable of providing yourself with the approval you seek, your need for external validation will start to vanish, leaving you stronger, more confident, and yes, happier in your life. Imagine how much time we lose each moment we restrain our authentic selves in an effort to be liked.

P6

If we base our worth on the opinions of others, we cheat ourselves of the power to shape our experiences and embrace life not only for others but also for ourselves, because ultimately, there is no difference. So embrace the cliché(老话) and love yourself as it's highly doubtful that you'll regret it.

46.答案 C推断题展开解析
判型

题干What can we conclude from Lao Zi's quotation?含conclude，要求由引语推出结论，而非复述字面。

拆句

题干What can we conclude from Lao Zi's quotation?是常规 wh- 疑问句，主干为 What can we conclude，from 介词短语修饰 conclude，结构平直，无陷阱。

定位
P1

Lao Zi once said, "Care about what other people think and you will always be their prisoner."

老子曾说：在意别人怎么想，你就会永远做他们的囚徒。

选项
A偷换概念

see through other people's attempt to make a prisoner of us把 prisoner 当成真有人想囚禁我们，原文是比喻、不是有人主动加害。

B张冠李戴

We can never really please other people even if we try as hard as we can.说的是讨好不可能成功，这是If it's true that you can't please all people all of the time的内容，不是P1老子那句引语的结论。

C✓ 正确
Care about what other people think↔taking to heart others' opinion of us
you will always be their prisoner↔can never be truly free

We can never be truly free if taking to heart others' opinion of us.同时覆盖了原文 if-then 链条的两端，是定位句的完整同义改写。

D无中生有

as much as they care about our own提出一种对等关心的主张，但P1引语只说在意他人会失自由，根本未涉及对方是否在意我们。

47.答案 B细节题展开解析
判型

题干What will happen if we base our self-worth on other people's approval?问的是一种行为的直接后果，答案应来自原文对应描述句的客观转述。

拆句

题干What will happen if we base our self-worth on other people's approval?是 if 引导条件状语从句的疑问句，主干 What will happen，base A on B 是固定搭配，意为以B为基础。

定位
P2

People-pleasing, or seeking self-worth through others' approval, is unproductive and an exhausting way to go through life.

讨好他人，或者说通过他人的认可来寻求自我价值，是一种无成效且令人疲惫的活法。

选项
A与原文相反

原文将该行为定性为 unproductive、exhausting，选项Our desire to be loved will be fulfilled.却说愿望被满足，与原文极性相反。

B✓ 正确
unproductive↔unfruitful
an exhausting way to go through life↔exhausting

Our life will be unfruitful and exhausting.同时覆盖了原文两个评价形容词 unproductive 与 exhausting，正合预判。

C无中生有

For social beings who desire love and belonging是描述人之常情，原文并未说 social beings 这一身份会因 people-pleasing 而被影响。

D与原文相反

where we get into trouble is when our self-worth is dependent upon whether we win someone's approval or not说明此举使自我陷入麻烦，Our sense of self will be sharpened and enhanced.却说自我被强化，正好相反。

这种想被喜欢的需求可以追溯到我们还是孩子、完全依赖他人照料的时候……我们要明白，当我们极度渴望得到他人认可时，背后真正驱动的，是那个仍然害怕被遗弃的小小孩子。

A偷换概念

We learn about who we are and what is expected of us based on interactions with others里 interactions 是孩子学习自我的途径，不是渴望被喜欢的成因。

B偷换概念

they are also trying to learn how the world works说孩子在学习世界运转方式，这是孩子在做的事，不是想被喜欢的成因。

knowledge about the pain of abandonment说我们已经知道被遗弃的痛苦；原文实际说的是儿童害怕被遗弃 still terrified of abandonment，是恐惧而非已掌握的知识。

when we were children↔early childhood

49.答案 A细节题展开解析
判型

题干What can we do when we become better able to provide ourselves with the desired approval?问的是某条件下发生的事/可获得的结果，属直接的客观陈述。

拆句

题干What can we do when we become better able to provide ourselves with the desired approval?主句是 What can we do，when 引导时间状语从句。从句主干 we become…able to…，better 修饰形容词 able。provide sb. with sth. 是固定搭配，意为给…提供…。

定位
P5

As you become more capable of providing yourself with the approval you seek, your need for external validation will start to vanish, leaving you stronger, more confident, and yes, happier in your life.

当你越来越能给自己所寻求的认可时，你对外部认可的需要会开始消退，让你变得更强、更自信、并且——是的——在生活中更幸福。

选项
A✓ 正确
happier in your life↔happier life

Enjoy a happier life.精准对应三个并列结果中的最后一项 happier in your life，且最直白、无歧义。

B与原文相反

原文we restrain our authentic selves是把克制真实自我描述为浪费时间的行为，不是该条件下我们应做的事；Exercise self-restraint.反而把原文要批判的行为当成了结果。

C与原文相反

your need for external validation will start to vanish明确说对外部认可的需求会消退，Receive more external validation.却说获得更多，方向相反。

D偷换概念

Imagine how much time we lose…是作者修辞性祈使，引导读者去想象浪费的时间，并不是说我们会去强化想象力。

50.答案 D细节题展开解析

拆句

题干What does the author advise us to do in the last paragraph?是 wh- 疑问句，主干 What does the author advise us to do，advise sb. to do sth. 是固定搭配，in the last paragraph 是地点状语限定范围。

定位
P6

So embrace the cliché(老话) and love yourself as it's highly doubtful that you'll regret it.

所以拥抱这句老话——爱自己吧，你几乎不可能后悔。

选项
A偷换概念

embrace life not only for others but also for ourselves说不仅为他人也为自己拥抱生活，是过渡论述，不是末段 So 之后给出的最终建议；选项Embrace life for ourselves and for others.把它包装成建议，越权代替了真正的祈使句。

B与原文相反

C无中生有

D✓ 正确
love yourself↔Love ourselves as we are

Love ourselves as we are.忠实保留了原文祈使动作 love+反身代词的结构，是末段建议的直译版。

第 2 篇 · 第 51–55 题

6 段 · 5 题
P1

Some people have said aging is more a slide into forgetfulness than a journey towards wisdom. However, a growing body of research suggests that late-in-life learning is possible. In reality, education does an aging brain good.

P2

Throughout life, people's brains constantly renovate themselves. In the late 1960s, British brain scientist Geoffrey Raisman spied growth in damaged brain regions of rats through an electron microscope; their brains were forging new connections. This meant brains may change every time a person learns something new.

P3

Of course, that doesn't mean the brain isn't affected by the effects of time. Just as height usually declines over the years, so does brain volume: Humans lose about 4 percent every decade starting in their 40s. But that reduction doesn't necessarily make people think slower; as long as we are alive and functioning, we can alter our brains with new information and experiences.

P4

In fact, scientists now suspect accumulating novel experiences, facts, and skills can keep people's minds more flexible. New pathways can strengthen our ever-changing mental structure, even as the brain shrinks.

P5

Conventional fixes like word puzzles and brain-training apps can contribute to mental durability. Even something as simple as taking a different route to the grocery store or going somewhere new on vacation can keep the brain healthy.

P6

A desire for new life challenges can further boost brainpower. Research about aging adults who take on new enterprises shows improved function and memory as well as a reduced risk of mental disease. Openness—a characteristic defined by curiosity and a desire for knowledge—may also help folks pass brain tests. Some folks are born with this take-in-the-world attitude, but those who aren't as genetically gifted aren't necessarily out of luck. While genes can encourage an interest in doing new things, a 2012 study in the journal Psychology and Aging found completing reasoning tasks like puzzles and number games can enhance that desire for novel experiences, which can, in turn, refresh the brain. That's why brain scientist Richard Kennedy says "It's not that old dogs can't learn new tricks. It's that maybe old dogs don't realize why they should."

51.答案 B细节题展开解析
判型

What do some people think of aging adults? 用 what do ... think of 直接问看法，且明确限定主语 some people，属于对原文具体观点的事实复述。

拆句

Q51 主干为 What do some people think of aging adults，think of 表示「对……的看法」，think 与 of 分立但语义绑定，基础薄弱考生易把 think of 拆开理解为「想到」，注意它是「评价」之义。

定位
P1

Some people have said aging is more a slide into forgetfulness than a journey towards wisdom.

某些人说，衰老与其说是一段通向智慧的旅程，不如说是一步步滑向健忘。

选项
A与原文相反

题干问 some people 的看法，原文 some people 恰恰用 more A than B 否定了「通向智慧」这一端，Their wisdom grows as time goes by. 正好与之相反。

B✓ 正确
a slide into forgetfulness↔memory gradually deteriorates

预判「记性变差」与 Their memory gradually deteriorates. 的语义完全一致，且整句都被原文 slide into forgetfulness 覆盖，无添加成分。

C张冠李戴

They can benefit from late-in-life learning. 是作者及研究方的观点（a growing body of research suggests that late-in-life learning is possible），不属于题干所问的 some people。

D无中生有

首段并未提及 some people 认为衰老会导致 mental health issues（精神健康问题），原文只说「健忘」（forgetfulness），健忘不等于心理疾病。

52.答案 C推断题展开解析
判型

What can we conclude from Geoffrey Raisman's finding? 出现 conclude，要求从已述事实再推一步得出结论。

拆句

Q52 句中 what 是 conclude 的宾语，from Geoffrey Raisman's finding 是状语，结构平铺直叙，无陷阱。

定位
P2

In the late 1960s, British brain scientist Geoffrey Raisman spied growth in damaged brain regions of rats through an electron microscope; their brains were forging new connections. This meant brains may change every time a person learns something new.

上世纪 60 年代末，英国脑科学家 Geoffrey Raisman 用电子显微镜观察到老鼠受损脑区长出新生组织；它们的大脑正在缔结新连接。这意味着，每当一个人学到新东西，大脑都可能发生改变。

选项
A无中生有

原文只说 Raisman 在「受损脑区」观察到新连接，并未谈论 brain damage 对 learning 的影响，更没有 hinders 这种负向判断。

B无中生有

原文未做「比想象慢/快」的速度比较，brain power weakens slower 这种比较关系在 Raisman 段并不存在。

C✓ 正确
brains may change every time a person learns something new↔Brains can refresh and improve with learning.

预判核心是「学习—大脑改变/更新」，Brains can refresh and improve with learning. 用 refresh、improve 概括 change + forging new connections 的双层含义，且 with learning 直接对应「每当学新东西」，整句无溢出。

D答非所问

Brains forge connections under new conditions. 几乎照搬 their brains were forging new connections，但题干问的是 conclude（结论意义），而该选项只重复了实验事实，没把意义说出来。

53.答案 D细节题展开解析
判型

What is one thing that helps maintain the health of our brain 用 one thing that ... 直接问一件具体做法，属事实复述。

拆句

Q53 主干 What is one thing，其后 that ... 是定语从句修饰 thing；even as it shrinks 是让步状语，as 此处等于 although，it 指代 brain，须留意 it 的指代关系不要错认成 health。

定位
P5

Even something as simple as taking a different route to the grocery store or going somewhere new on vacation can keep the brain healthy.

哪怕是像换条路去超市、或换个地方度假这样简单的事，也能让大脑保持健康。

选项
A与原文相反

B无中生有

Avoiding worrying about our mental durability. 提到「避免担忧脑力耐久度」，但原文 P5 谈的是「具体做法」，从未谈论「担忧」或「避免担忧」这个心理层面。

C偷换概念

原文末段 It\'s not that old dogs can\'t learn new tricks. It\'s that maybe old dogs don\'t realize why they should. 中的 old dogs 是比喻人，不是真的「狗的学习方式」；Imitating old dogs\' way of learning new tricks. 把比喻当字面，且并非 P5 的护脑手段。

D✓ 正确
taking a different route to the grocery store or going somewhere new on vacation↔Approaching everyday tasks in novel ways.

预判的两个关键语义点「日常事」和「新方式」在 Approaching everyday tasks in novel ways. 中一一对应，没有任何额外成分。

54.答案 A细节题展开解析
判型

What does the author say can contribute to the improvement of brain function? 直接问 author 提出的「促进项」，是对原文表述的事实复述。

拆句

Q54 主干为 What does the author say can contribute to ...，what 既作 say 的宾语，又作 can contribute to 的逻辑主语（省略关系代词的嵌套从句结构），基础薄弱考生易在两个谓语间断错语义，建议拆为「作者说，[什么]能促进脑功能提升」。

定位
P6

Openness—a characteristic defined by curiosity and a desire for knowledge—may also help folks pass brain tests.

「开放性」——一种由好奇心和求知欲所定义的特质——也可以帮助人们顺利通过脑力测试。

选项
A✓ 正确
curiosity and a desire for knowledge↔Being curious and desiring knowledge.

预判的两个核心要素「好奇心」和「求知欲」在 Being curious and desiring knowledge. 中由同根词分别承接，且选项整体只表达这两点，无多余成分，无遗漏。

B偷换概念

原文说 openness 能帮助 pass brain tests，是「特质 → 通过测试」的因果；Being eager to pass brain tests at an old age. 把「通过测试」偷换成「渴望通过测试」，把结果偷换成动机，方向不同。

C无中生有

原文 A desire for new life challenges can further boost brainpower. 只说「迎接新挑战」能提升脑力，从未提到 avoiding risks（规避风险）；C 项硬加了「规避风险」这一成分。

D偷换概念

原文 improved function and memory as well as a reduced risk of mental disease 中，reduced risk of mental disease 是 take on new enterprises 带来的「结果之一」，而非「促进脑功能的手段」；Boosting immunity to serious mental diseases. 把这一结果倒置成促进手段。

55.答案 C细节题展开解析
判型

What is the finding of the 2012 study 直接问研究发现内容，属定位+复述型问法。

拆句

Q55 主干为 What is the finding，of the 2012 study 与 in the journal Psychology and Aging 都是修饰 finding 的介词短语，结构平铺直叙，无陷阱。

定位
P6

While genes can encourage an interest in doing new things, a 2012 study in the journal Psychology and Aging found completing reasoning tasks like puzzles and number games can enhance that desire for novel experiences, which can, in turn, refresh the brain.

尽管基因能在一定程度上鼓励一个人对新事物的兴趣，但 2012 年发表在《Psychology and Aging》期刊上的一项研究发现，完成像谜题、数字游戏这样的推理任务能够强化「想要新体验」的欲望，而这种欲望反过来又能刷新大脑。

选项
A偷换概念

原文是「做推理任务（含 puzzles）→ 强化对新体验的欲望 → 刷新大脑」，Wishing to solve puzzles enhances one\'s reasoning power. 反过来变成「想做谜题 → 提升推理力」，方向被颠倒，且原文从未谈 enhance reasoning power。

B无中生有

原文链条终点是 refresh the brain（刷新大脑），不是 stimulates one\'s memory（刺激记忆）；且 unexpectedly（出乎意料地）也不在原文里。

C✓ 正确
that desire for novel experiences, which can, in turn, refresh the brain↔Desiring new experiences can help to renovate the brain.

预判的落脚点「对新体验的欲望能刷新大脑」与 Desiring new experiences can help to renovate the brain. 在主语、动作、对象上一一对位，无添加成分。

D张冠李戴

Learning new tricks should not be confined to old dogs only. 改写自结尾的 Richard Kennedy 引语，是 brain scientist Kennedy 的比喻金句，而非 2012 年那项研究的 finding。
