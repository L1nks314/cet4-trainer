题型：给下面 10 个句子各配一个段落字母（A–O）。 先读句子划关键词，再回原文定位。原文见左侧，题目在右侧。

The battle over bike lanes needs a mindset shift

15 段 · 10 题
A

Five years ago, the city of Queens, New York, announced that it would be putting bike lanes onto a stretch of Skillman Ave — and removing 116 parking spots. Cyclists loved the plan, but local business owners got very angry. Taking out those parking spots, as they argued at protests and in letters to the city council, would ruin stores and restaurants along Skillman. "Parking here is already a nightmare," one shouted at a protest rally.

五年前，纽约皇后区宣布将在Skillman Ave的一段路上铺设自行车道，并移除116个停车位。骑自行车的人喜欢这个计划，但当地企业主非常生气。正如他们在抗议活动和致市议会的信中所争论的那样，拆除这些停车位将破坏Skillman沿线的商店和餐馆。有人在抗议集会上喊道： “在这里停车已经是一场噩梦。”

B

But the bike lanes were a done deal, and soon they were in place. Early this year, Jesse Coburn — an investigative writer with Streetsblog New York — wondered whether those predictions of economic collapse came true. So he asked the city's Department of Finance to give him a few years' worth of sales figures for that stretch of Skillman Ave. How had the businesses on that street fared?

但自行车道已经完成了交易，很快就到位了。今年早些时候，纽约街头博客的调查作家杰西·科伯恩（ Jesse Coburn ）想知道这些经济崩溃的预测是否成真。因此，他要求该市的财政部门给他几年的销售数据，用于Skillman Ave的延伸。那条街上的生意怎么样了？

C

Quite well, it turns out. In the year after the bike lanes arrived, businesses on Skillman saw sales rise by 12 percent, compared to 3 percent for Queens in general. What's more, that section of road saw new businesses open, while Queens overall had a net loss.

事实证明，这很好。在自行车道到达后的一年中， Skillman的企业销售额增长了12% ，而皇后区的整体销售额增长了3%。更重要的是，这段道路开辟了新业务，而皇后区整体上出现了净亏损。

D

The thing is, the actual merchants along Skillman? They didn't believe it. When Coburn spoke to them and described what he'd found, only a few store owners admitted the lanes had helped. Many still insisted the lanes were killing their part of the city. And emotions ran hot: Someone scattered tacks(大头钉) on the bike lane.

问题是，沿着Skillman的实际商人？他们不相信。当Coburn与他们交谈并描述他的发现时，只有少数商店老板承认这些车道有所帮助。许多人仍然坚持认为这些车道正在杀死他们所在的城市。情绪很激动：有人在自行车大头钉道上撒了钉子。

E

This little story turns out to be a fascinating glimpse at the challenges cities face as they try to update their urban infrastructure — to clean up the air, reduce greenhouse emissions, and speed up travel by making towns more bike-friendly. There's a rising amount of data showing that installing bike lanes and making streets more pedestrian-friendly boosts the economic fortunes of a place. Removing cars and parking spots works. But the folks who run local businesses simply aren't convinced, even when their own street performs. Given that sort of mess, can political fights over bike lanes ever end?

这个小故事原来是一个迷人的一瞥，在城市试图更新其城市基础设施时所面临的挑战—清洁空气，减少温室气体排放，并通过使城镇更适合骑自行车来加速旅行。越来越多的数据显示，安装自行车道和使街道对行人更加友好可以提升一个地方的经济财富。拆卸汽车和停车位是有效的。但经营当地企业的人根本不相信，即使他们自己的街道表现出色。鉴于这种混乱，围绕自行车道的政治斗争能否结束？

F

In 2013, researchers at New York City's Department of Transportation studied seven stretches of road that had installed bike lanes or created pedestrian-friendly areas. The city analyzed the data for businesses along those routes and found that by the third year, sales grew faster on five of the streets than in the district overall, on average — up to five times faster, in fact.

2013年，纽约市交通部的研究人员研究了七条安装了自行车道或创建了行人友好区域的道路。该市分析了这些路线沿线企业的数据，发现到第三年，五条街道的销售额增长速度平均高于整个地区—实际上高达五倍。

G

Beyond New York, a survey of research from 23 cities found that bike lanes and pedestrian-friendly design didn't hurt local retail and food stores. ("Fears of disastrous consequences for local businesses are unfounded," the researchers concluded.) More recent work has shown roughly the same.

在纽约以外，一项针对23个城市的调查发现，自行车道和对行人友好的设计并没有损害当地的零售和食品店。（研究人员总结道： “对当地企业灾难性后果的担忧是没有根据的。” ）最近的研究表明大致相同。

H

The truth is that in fairly dense areas, bikes are more efficient at moving people around. You might lose one car driver's business — but you gain shoppers who now can arrive more easily on bikes. "Cyclists and pedestrians are consumers too," notes Professor Susan Handy. Plus, streets redesigned for bikes and pedestrians tend to become more pleasant places to hang around, so "in a lot of cases, that's created much nicer environments that are really good for those businesses."

事实是，在相当密集的地区，自行车可以更有效地运送人员。您可能会失去一位汽车司机的业务，但您会吸引更多的购物者，他们现在可以更轻松地骑自行车到达。Susan Handy教授指出： “骑自行车的人和行人也是消费者。”此外，为自行车和行人重新设计的街道往往会成为更舒适的闲逛场所，因此“在很多情况下，这创造了更好的环境，对这些企业非常有利。”

I

Mom-and-pop shops are usually pretty quick at recognizing situations that will help their bottom lines. So why the blind spot here? Perhaps it's that attention focuses on horror stories — and some merchants do get hit when bike lanes come in.

夫妻店通常很快就能识别出有助于他们盈利的情况。那为什么是盲点呢？也许是因为注意力集中在恐怖故事上--当自行车道出现时，一些商家确实会受到打击。

J

I spoke to Cindy Hughes, a hairdresser(理发师) in Cambridge, Massachusetts. She said business fell by at least 40 percent when the city removed nearby parking to put in a bike lane. The majority of her customers drive, with many coming from nearby towns. Only a very few have shifted to cycling, and even those almost certainly won't cycle in Boston's snowy winters. So while Hughes supports bike lanes — "cyclists deserve to be safe" — she sees the parking loss as an existential risk. "Look, 90 percent of my customers drive," she told me. "For our business, the bike lanes are way worse than Covid was."

我采访了马萨诸塞州剑桥的理发师辛迪·休斯（ Cindy Hughes理发师 ）。她说，当该市将附近的停车场移至自行车道时，业务至少下降了40 ％。她的大多数顾客都开车，其中许多人来自附近的城镇。只有极少数人转向骑自行车，即使是那些几乎肯定不会在波士顿多雪的冬天骑自行车的人。因此，虽然休斯支持自行车道— “骑自行车的人应该安全” —但她认为停车损失是一种存在的风险。“听着， 90%的顾客都在开车，”她告诉我。“对于我们的业务来说，自行车道比新冠肺炎疫情要糟糕得多。”

K

For others, the pushback is cultural, says Henry Grabar, a writer for the Slate Magazine. Small business owners are frequently drivers who commute from other parts of the city by car, Grabar points out. They're also often longtime locals. "They tend to be people with deep roots in the city, who have hung around since before the neighborhood became what it is today," he adds. Driving around town in a car is so normal to them that cycling seems weird and unusual — despite its boost from Covid, when bike sales exploded by 75 percent.

Slate杂志的作者亨利·格拉巴尔（ Henry Grabar ）说，对于其他人来说，这种抵制是文化上的。Grabar指出，小企业主通常是开车从城市其他地方通勤的司机。他们通常也是长期居住的当地人。他补充说： “他们往往是在城市中根深蒂固的人，他们在社区成为今天的样子之前就一直在附近闲逛。”在城里开车对他们来说是如此平常，以至于骑自行车似乎很奇怪和不寻常--尽管它从新冠肺炎疫情中得到了提升，当时自行车销量激增了75%。

L

Psychology beats all! Who knew, right? The fierce divide between store owners and bike-lane advocates seems similar to our larger culture wars over climate change. If we've learned anything about culture wars, it's that data isn't much good at changing minds.

心理学胜过所有人！谁知道，对吧？店主和自行车道拥护者之间的激烈分歧似乎与我们在气候变化方面的更大文化战争相似。如果我们对文化战争有所了解，那就是数据不太擅长改变主意。

M

When Janette Sadik-Khan was the head of New York City's Transportation Department back in the early 2000s, she oversaw a rollout(推出) of bike lanes — and got angry blowback from residents and business owners who claimed there weren't enough cyclists to justify installing lanes. Now, she notes ironically, the lanes are so full of activity that opponents have turned to claiming the problem is the opposite: There are too many cyclists getting in the way of cars.

21世纪初，珍妮特·萨迪克—汗（ Janette Sadik-Khan ）担任纽约市交通局局长时，她监督了自行车道（推出 ）的推出，并遭到居民和企业主的愤怒反击，他们声称自行车骑行者不足以证明安装车道是合理的。现在，她指出，具有讽刺意味的是，车道上充斥着各种活动，以至于对手们转而声称问题恰恰相反：有太多的骑自行车的人挡住了汽车的路。

N

Maybe bike lanes will always be charged with emotion, until enough of the public is finally concerned about climate change — and it seems reckless(鲁莽的) to not have them.

也许自行车道总是充满情感，直到有足够多的公众最终关注气候变化--而没有自行车道鲁莽的似乎是鲁莽的。

O

Crises, after all, have a way of opening people's eyes to possibilities. During Covid, restaurants and cafés lost so much business that cities nationwide began allowing them to build curbside seating areas where people could sit, safely, in the open air. It greatly reduced parking — but because, well, shop owners didn't see any way around it. Customers loved the outdoor seating so much that cities are making it permanent: A New York City study of several streets closed during Covid found storeowners making more than before, and diners enjoying the outdoor lifestyle. If data won't change minds, customers might.

毕竟，危机有一种方式可以让人们对可能性睁开眼睛。在新冠肺炎疫情期间，餐馆和咖啡馆失去了如此多的业务，以至于全国的城市开始允许他们在路边建造座位区，让人们可以安全地坐在露天。它大大减少了停车位，但由于店主没有看到任何解决办法。顾客非常喜欢户外座位，以至于城市正在将其永久化：纽约市对新冠肺炎疫情期间关闭的几条街道的研究发现，商店老板的收入比以前多，用餐者享受户外生活方式。如果数据不会改变主意，客户可能会改变主意。
