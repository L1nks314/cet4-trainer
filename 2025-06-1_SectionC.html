短文一。
Passage 1.
说话是人类独有的能力。
Talking is unique to humans.
一种动物可能发出 10 种不同的声音，而一个成年人认识 2 万多个单词。
An animal might make 10 different sounds, but an adult human knows more than 20,000 words.
此外，我们是唯一用完整句子表达思想的动物。
Additionally, we're the only animal that expresses thoughts in full sentences.
研究人员认为语言不是被发明出来的。
Researchers don't think language was invented.
他们认为语言是进化而来的。
Instead, they think it evolved.
主要有两种理论。
There are two main theories.
第一种理论认为，语言始于人们发出各种不同的声音，主要是模仿周围的事物，比如动物的叫声、大自然的声音和工具的声响。
The first theory is that language started with people making different sounds, mostly imitating the things around them like animal calls, nature sounds, and the sounds of tools.
也许他们用风声来评论天气，或者模仿鸟叫来告诉朋友附近有只鸟。
Perhaps they made the sound of wind to comment on the weather, or imitated the sound of a bird to tell a friend that there was a bird nearby.
然后，经过数十万年，这些声音变成了词语，人们开始把它们作为自己语言的一部分来学习。
Then, over hundreds of thousands of years, those sounds turned into words that people began to learn as part of their language.
在某个时候，人们开始把词语连在一起组成句子。
At some point, people started stringing the words together to form sentences.
另一种主要理论出现得更晚，它认为人类最初是通过手势交流的，用手指着东西，用身体模仿动作。
The other main theory, which is more recent, is that people started off by gesturing, pointing at things with their hands, and imitating actions using their bodies.
最终，这些手势发展成了完整的手语。
Eventually, these gestures turned into full sign language.
这一理论推测，在发展出手语之后，人们最终开始一边打手势一边发出声音。
This theory guesses that after developing sign languages, people eventually started making sounds along with their gestures.
在某个时候，他们转而主要发出后来变成词语的声音，而不再只是用身体表达。
At some point, they switched to mostly making sounds that became words instead of just using their bodies.
该理论认为，他们之所以转而发声，是因为大声说话能让你在看不见对方的情况下也能与之交流。
The reason they switched to making sounds, the theory goes, is that talking out loud lets you communicate with someone even when you can't see them.
短文二。
Passage 2.
展示奢侈品牌和其他身份标志是个好主意吗？
Is it a good idea to display luxury brands and other signals of status?
这要看情况。
It depends on the situation.
研究表明，看起来富有的人往往会被认为比那些不富有的人更聪明、更自律、更有能力。
Studies have shown that people who appear to be wealthy tend to be considered more intelligent, disciplined, and competent than those who do not.
但一项新研究发现，人们认为炫耀自己社会地位的人更在意自身利益，而不是帮助他人。
But new research found that people believe someone who shows off their social status cares more about benefiting themselves than helping others.
这意味着人们不太愿意与他们合作。
This means people are less willing to collaborate with them.
在合作至关重要时，谦逊也许很关键，但研究人员也发现，在某些情况下，彰显地位是有好处的。
Modesty may be key when cooperation is essential, but the researchers also found that in some cases, status signaling has advantages.
实验表明，在为一个寻求合作成员的团队招募人选时，参与者不太可能选择炫耀财富或地位的人；但当他们在寻找有竞争力的团队成员时，却更可能选择这样的人。
Experiments showed that participants were less likely to choose someone who signals their wealth or status to join a group seeking cooperative members, but participants were more likely to choose that person when they were looking for a competitive team member.
这些发现表明，人们应当根据自己社交目标的不同来改变自我展现的方式。
These findings suggest that people should change how they present themselves depending on their social goal.
这是社交媒体的时代。
This is the era of social media.
人们可以轻易地向大量受众展示自己的财富和地位，但他们需要考虑到后果。
People can easily share their wealth and status to large audiences, but they need to consider the consequences.
在网上发布购买奢侈品和奢华度假的内容，也许能帮你说服别人、震慑对手，但它也可能向潜在的朋友或未来的雇主表明，你不太会考虑他人的需求。
Posting about luxury purchases and expensive vacations online may help you to persuade others and frighten competitors, but it could also signal to potential friends or future employers that you are unlikely to think about the needs of others.
这让那些既想给人留下深刻印象、又想表现出自己是团队合作者的人处境很棘手。
This makes things tricky for people who may want to impress others while also demonstrating that they are a team player.
短文三。
Passage 3.
人类对动物的着迷和人类的历史一样久远。
Human fascination with animals goes back as far as humans do.
在已发现的最古老的洞穴壁画中，有些距今长达 4 万年，其中动物的形象比人类还多。
Of the oldest cave paintings discovered, some are up to 40,000 years old and there are more images of animals than humans.
在某个时候，人类开始捕捉并圈养动物。
At some point, humans began to capture and hold animals.
已知最早的动物收藏由王室所有，并不向公众开放。
The first known collections were held by royalty and were not open to the public.
在埃及，研究人员发现了约公元前 3500 年的建筑，里面保存着动物遗骸，其中包括并非埃及本土的大象。
In Egypt, researchers have found buildings from around 3500 BC containing the remains of animals including elephants which were not native to Egypt.
但这些动物的生活并不轻松。
But life wasn't easy for these animals.
它们可能寿命很短，遗骸上还有严重受伤的痕迹。
They probably had short lives and the remains show evidence of severe injuries.
最早的动物公开展览，可能是由埃及女王哈特谢普苏特在约公元前 1480 年创办的。
The first public exhibit of animals may have been created by Egypt's Queen Hatshepsut around 1480 BC.
研究人员认为，这座动物园最初饲养的是从一个叫做蓬特的遥远地方带回的动物。
Researchers think the zoo was started with animals brought home from a far-off land known as Point.
女王为何建造这座动物园尚不清楚，但也许是为了炫耀自己的财富和权力。
It's unclear why the queen built the zoo but it might have been to show off her wealth and power.
世界各地的早期动物园都有发现。
Early zoos are found all over the world.
在中国，据说周文王在约公元前 1060 年修建了一座「灵囿」。
In China, Emperor Wen Wang is said to have built a Garden of Intelligence around 1060 BC.
里面养着鹿、鸟和许多鱼。
It included deer, birds and many fish.
在英格兰，国王亨利一世在大约 1110 年建立了一处动物收藏，作为王室领地的一部分。
In England, King Henry I set up a collection of animals in about 1110 as part of the royal estate.
他的收藏中有老虎和狮子。
His collection included tigers and lions.
这批动物最终于 1235 年迁到了伦敦塔。
This collection eventually moved to the Tower of London in 1235.
这批动物在那个地方留存了 600 年。
The collection stayed at that location for 600 years.
